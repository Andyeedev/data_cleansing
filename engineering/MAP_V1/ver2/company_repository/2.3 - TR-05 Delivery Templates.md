---

# 1. Purpose

The Delivery Template Library provides a standardized collection of templates used to execute approved migration, transformation, modernization, and product delivery initiatives.

The templates ensure delivery activities are repeatable, governed, measurable, and traceable from planning through successful completion.

---

# 2. Objectives

The Delivery Template Library aims to:

* Standardize delivery execution.
* Improve delivery quality and consistency.
* Support governance throughout implementation.
* Reduce execution risk.
* Improve collaboration across delivery teams.
* Capture delivery evidence for assurance and audit.
* Enable repeatable migration and transformation practices.
* Maintain traceability between planning, execution, and operational handover.

---

# 3. Scope

The templates support delivery across:

* Migration execution
* Cloud transformation
* Infrastructure deployment
* Application migration
* Data migration
* Business process transition
* Testing and validation
* Cutover execution
* Operational readiness
* Service transition
* Benefits realization

# 5. Standard Template Structure

Each delivery template should contain:

## Document Information

* Template ID
* Template Name
* Version
* Owner
* Approval Status
* Effective Date
* Review Date

---

## Purpose

Defines the objective of the delivery activity.

---

## When to Use

Identifies the stage of execution when the template should be completed.

---

## Intended Audience

Typical users include:

* Delivery Managers
* Project Managers
* Programme Managers
* Product Managers
* Product Owners
* Solution Architects
* Enterprise Architects
* Migration Engineers
* Test Managers
* Service Transition Managers
* Operations Teams
* PMO

---

## Required Inputs

Typical inputs include:

* Approved Delivery Plan
* Product Roadmap
* Migration Roadmap
* Architecture Blueprint
* Risk Register
* Governance Approval
* Resource Plan
* Communications Plan

---

## Instructions

Provides guidance for completing the template, mandatory fields, governance requirements, and examples where appropriate.

---

## Template Body

Structured fields, checklists, logs, tables, records, schedules, and evidence to support delivery execution.

---

## Review & Approval

Defines required reviewers, approvers, and quality gates before progressing to the next delivery stage.

---

## Related Documents

References associated Product Canon, Blueprint Library, Planning Templates, Governance Templates, Risk Templates, and Operations Templates.

# 6. Delivery Lifecycle

Delivery activities typically follow this sequence:

1. Confirm governance approval.
2. Review implementation plan.
3. Allocate resources.
4. Execute delivery work packages.
5. Perform testing and validation.
6. Conduct readiness assessment.
7. Execute cutover.
8. Validate production environment.
9. Transition to operations.
10. Capture lessons learned.
11. Complete delivery closure.

---

# 7. Design Principles

Delivery templates should be:

* Operationally practical
* Repeatable
* Evidence-driven
* Traceable
* Risk-aware
* Collaborative
* Automation-friendly
* Vendor-neutral
* Audit-ready
* Outcome-focused

---

# 8. Governance

The Delivery Management Office is responsible for:

* Maintaining delivery templates.
* Reviewing updates.
* Ensuring alignment with the Product Canon and Blueprint Library.
* Publishing approved versions.
* Retiring obsolete templates.

# 10. Template Relationships

Delivery Templates consume outputs from:

* Strategy Templates (TR-02)
* Discovery Templates (TR-03)
* Planning Templates (TR-04)

They provide inputs to:

* Governance Templates (TR-06)
* Risk & Compliance Templates (TR-07)
* Operations Templates (TR-09)
* Measurement & Reporting Templates (TR-10)
* Portfolio Templates (TR-11)

Delivery evidence forms part of the Migration Assurance Platform audit trail, supporting governance, assurance, compliance, and customer reporting.

---

# 11. Success Measures

The Delivery Template Library is successful when:

* Delivery activities follow approved standards.
* Migration execution is consistent across engagements.
* Delivery evidence supports governance and assurance.
* Operational handovers are complete and documented.
* Delivery risks and issues are managed proactively.
* Lessons learned are captured and reused.
* Delivery outcomes remain traceable to approved plans and business objectives.
