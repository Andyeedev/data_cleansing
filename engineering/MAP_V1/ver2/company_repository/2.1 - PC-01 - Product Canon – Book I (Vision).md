# Chapter 1 – Company Identity

## 1.1 Purpose

This chapter defines the permanent identity of the company and its flagship product. It establishes the official terminology and positioning that must be used consistently across all future documentation, applications, presentations and customer engagements.

---

## 1.2 Company Purpose

Our purpose is to reduce the complexity, risk and uncertainty associated with enterprise database migration projects by providing a platform that delivers repeatable, auditable and automated migration assurance.

Rather than replacing migration tools or consultancy services, the platform provides the assurance layer that enables organisations to execute migration programmes with greater confidence, traceability and governance.

---

## 1.3 Vision

> **To become the trusted Migration Intelligence Platform for enterprise modernisation, enabling organisations to migrate critical data with confidence through automation, evidence and intelligent decision support.**

### Why this wording?

Notice what it doesn't say.

* Not Azure.
* Not AI.
* Not validation.
* Not PostgreSQL.
* Not cloud.

A vision should remain true even if the underlying technology changes.

---

## 1.4 Mission

> **To build a metadata-driven platform that transforms complex migration activities into repeatable, automated and auditable processes, reducing delivery risk while accelerating enterprise modernisation.**

Unlike the Vision, the Mission describes what we do every day.

---

## 1.5 Positioning Statement

This is the single sentence that should appear consistently across proposals, cloud applications and marketing material.

> **We develop a Migration Intelligence Platform that provides automated migration assurance for enterprise database modernisation programmes.**

That sentence deliberately avoids mentioning implementation details while clearly communicating the category we occupy.

---

## 1.6 Product Definition

This is one area where I recommend we be very deliberate.

During Phase 1, we repeatedly referred to the software as a "Validation Engine." Technically that's correct—but strategically it's limiting.

The platform should be defined as follows:

| Level                | Official Name                       | Purpose                                                |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Company              | *Your Company*                      | Organisation developing the platform                   |
| Platform             | **Migration Intelligence Platform** | The complete product vision                            |
| Current Product      | **Migration Assurance Platform**    | The commercially available product                     |
| Core Capability (V1) | **Validation Engine**               | Initial implementation and first production capability |

This distinction gives us room to grow without renaming the product every time we add a capability.

# Review of Book I

I think this first book is already stronger than anything we've produced previously because it establishes a **clear product hierarchy**. That hierarchy eliminates one of the biggest sources of confusion we had during Phase 1: treating the current implementation as the entire product.

There is, however, one strategic point I'd like us to discuss before we proceed to Book II.

## A naming decision

We now have three distinct concepts:

1. **Migration Intelligence Platform** – the long-term vision.
2. **Migration Assurance Platform** – the commercial product customers buy.
3. **Validation Engine** – the first implemented capability.

I believe this is architecturally and commercially sound because it separates the company vision from today's implementation.

Before we lock the Canon, I'd like us to decide whether **"Migration Assurance Platform"** is the right commercial product name or whether there's another name that better reflects your ambitions. Once we settle that, every future document—including Microsoft Founders Hub, investor packs, and your website—will use the same terminology consistently. I recommend making that decision now because changing it later would ripple through every deliverable.
